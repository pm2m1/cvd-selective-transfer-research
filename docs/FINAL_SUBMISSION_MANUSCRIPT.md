# Selective multiview transfer for wave-observed, doctor-reported heart attack or stroke across HAALSI and MHAS: a reproducibility study

**Final submission draft for author review, 26 October 2026.** This package changes reporting and presentation only. The historical manuscripts, frozen analyses and original figures remain unchanged. Authorship, affiliations, funding and conflict disclosures, venue format, and product-specific MHAS citations require author confirmation before submission. The experimental phase is closed; no further target-test access is authorized.

## Abstract

**Background.** A small labeled target cohort motivates selective transfer of a model trained elsewhere. We tested a re-engineered DMV-MAT-style selector in two longitudinal aging studies, asking whether adapting selected neural modules improves prediction of a *wave-observed, doctor-reported heart attack or stroke* relative to full fine-tuning. This is an application and reproducibility study, not a new algorithm claim.

**Methods.** HAALSI Wave 1 supplied 2,906 eligible source participants (107 later positive reports); the MHAS 2012 examination cohort supplied 1,133 target participants (83 positive reports). Ten prespecified baseline predictors formed five views. One deterministic target lock produced an adaptation pool of 679 (49 positive), a validation set of 171 (13 positive), and a held-out test set of 283 (21 positive). Ten overlapping, nested target adaptation draws used either 25 positive plus 75 negative or 10 plus 30 labeled participants. All compared methods shared the corresponding draw. The primary frozen comparison was calibrated-logical selective adaptation minus full fine-tuning in average precision (AP), averaged across ten paired primary-budget models on the *same* test people. A 2,000-replicate joint participant bootstrap supplied descriptive percentile intervals.

**Results.** Primary-budget mean held-out AP was 0.1427 for calibrated logical and 0.1408 for full fine-tuning; the prespecified paired difference was **+0.00182 (descriptive 95% interval −0.02481 to +0.03234)**. Head-only and parameter-matched random AP were 0.1424 and 0.1418. The logical selector adapted no additional module in nine of ten draws, yielding mean 0.51% trainable parameters versus 100% for full fine-tuning. Random and logical masks coincided in nine of ten primary draws and all ten low-label draws. Under 10+30 labels, the secondary logical-versus-full AP difference was +0.00794 (−0.01418 to +0.03276).

**Conclusions.** The held-out estimate establishes neither superiority nor equivalence. Sparse parameter use mostly reflected abstention that resembled head-only tuning; the matched-random control offered limited independent discrimination. The study documents transfer-method behavior for survey-reported outcomes among observed participants, without validating clinical incidence or individual risk.

## 1. Introduction

Cross-cohort prediction faces differences in measurements, participant mix, health systems, and the relationship between predictors and reported outcomes. Fully tuning a source-trained network on a small target label set may be unstable; tuning only its prediction head may preserve representations that need adaptation. Module selection proposes an intermediate choice. The original DMV-MAT work developed multiview module adaptation for EEG recognition, evaluating module transferability and deciding which weights to share [1]. This project applies that *general design principle* to cardiovascular survey data. The implemented selector equations, calibration and synthetic development protocol are re-engineered and **are not claimed as the original paper's exact algorithm**.

The frozen study asks whether `calibrated_logical` improves target AP relative to full fine-tuning, while also describing trainable-parameter use. It compares head-only tuning, fixed and random masks, legacy top-k, calibrated threshold, and tabular/neural reference methods. The endpoint is limited to new *reports* of a doctor-diagnosed heart attack or stroke at longitudinal survey waves. These events were not adjudicated with clinical records. We use TRIPOD+AI and STROBE as reporting references and PROBAST+AI as an appraisal framework [2–4]; a completed item-by-item checklist or formal risk-of-bias rating has not been established.

## 2. Related work and method provenance

The source DMV-MAT publication concerns subject-specific EEG tasks and proposes a deep multiview network with module adaptation based on source/target feature distributions [1]. Our five medically defined views, source-relative signed class geometry, permutation-calibrated multi-kernel MMD and independently thresholded logical rule were developed in this repository's synthetic milestones. The original method inspired **module-level SHARE/ADAPT decisions**; neither its exact equations nor its EEG performance are claimed here. The MMD statistic determines a mask; it is detached from gradients and is **not** a backpropagated domain-alignment loss.

Domain adaptation with distribution matching and domain-adversarial representation learning predates this study [15,16]. In particular, deep adaptation networks use MMD to help *train* representations [15], whereas our MMD is only a pretraining-frozen module-selection statistic. Parameter-efficient transfer also predates this work: adapter approaches update a small fraction of parameters in other model families [17], and SpotTune has investigated adaptive fine-tuning choices in computer vision [18]. Our frozen module mask differs from both adapter insertion and SpotTune's per-instance policy, but is not a demonstrated advance over either. Head-only and full fine-tuning are essential local controls. A matched-random mask was prespecified to ask whether *which* module was chosen matters beyond trainable-parameter count; its realized search space proved too small to make this a strong independent control. The contribution is a cross-cohort implementation and auditable negative/inconclusive comparison, rather than a novel transferable selection algorithm.

## 3. Data sources, population and outcome

HAALSI is a longitudinal aging study in rural South Africa [5,6]; MHAS is a longitudinal aging study in Mexico that included a 2012 biomarker examination subsample [7,8]. The analyzed releases and field-level evidence are specified in the [public protocol](PHASE1_METHOD_AND_REPRODUCIBILITY.md) and [evidence reconciliation](PHASE1_METHOD_AND_REPRODUCIBILITY.md). The selected analytic cohorts are not representative samples of all adults in either country: examination eligibility, complete predictors, prior-disease exclusion and observed follow-up restrict them. Data are held under the respective release terms; no participant-level data are reproduced here.

The source index was the HAALSI Wave-1 baseline collection episode. The target index was the MHAS 2012 examination date, restricted to **0–30 calendar days after** a completed baseline interview so examination-derived predictors were available at the landmark. Disease onset between interview and examination cannot always be ruled out. People reporting prior heart attack or stroke at baseline were excluded. The positive outcome is a new doctor-reported heart attack **or** stroke at the intermediate or final planned survey wave. A final observed negative with no earlier positive is a known negative under the prespecified observation rule. Death or noninterview without an earlier positive does not imply freedom from disease and is excluded from the primary binary analysis. Follow-up is wave-based, approximately six years; neither exact onset dates nor a precise 1,826-day first-event label are established.

MHAS harmonized Version-D disease indicators carry previous positive reports forward. HAALSI positive-to-negative reversals were handled at the disease-component level: explicit “never had,” “misdiagnosed,” or unexplained reversals were uncertain; “no longer has” did not erase an earlier positive; a separately valid positive of the other component retained the composite event. An intermediate positive followed by a missing final report remained a positive report under the frozen rule. This rule avoids treating every contradiction as a confirmed event or every final negative as proof that an earlier report was false. It remains a report-based endpoint, subject to recall, diagnosis access and measurement differences. Validation studies in Spain and Korea found different error patterns for self-reported MI and stroke [9,10], but those populations, questions and reference standards differ from HAALSI/MHAS; their predictive values cannot be imported as cohort-specific correction factors.

The final eligible HAALSI source cohort was **2,906 people / 107 positive reports**. The MHAS primary cohort was **1,133 / 83**. **Table 1** and [Figure 1](../figures/01_cohort_flow.svg) show the frozen partitions.

## 4. Predictors and five-view model

Ten prespecified predictors were harmonized across cohorts: background (age, baseline sex); haemodynamic (systolic and diastolic blood pressure); metabolic (BMI, total cholesterol, HDL); behaviour (smoking status); and comorbidity/medication (doctor-reported diabetes, antihypertensive treatment). The separately versioned nine-feature configuration omits diabetes because baseline doctor-report constructions differ. It was **not** trained as part of the frozen final registration, so no nine-feature test result is presented. No follow-up variable, outcome, death flag or identifier entered a model view. HAALSI lipids were converted from mmol/L to mg/dL under the approved mapping. Source-training medians and scales were applied unchanged to transfer models' target data; target-only methods fitted their own preprocessing on their adaptation draw. No validation or test feature was used for fitting. This harmonization does not prove measurement equivalence, particularly for diabetes and medication reports.

The existing network has five view-specific encoders, two fusion modules and a binary prediction head ([Figure 2](../figures/02_architecture_selector.svg)). Encoded view dimensions were 4, 2, 3, 4 and 6; the network had **8,033 parameters**. Each encoder is a Linear/ReLU/LayerNorm block of width 16; fusion widths are 64 and 32. Head-only tuning updates 33 parameters. All neural adaptations used the relevant frozen source checkpoint, binary cross-entropy with logits and AdamW. The architecture and selector definitions were frozen before test access. Figure 2 is an implementation diagram, not a reproduction of the original EEG network.

## 5. Re-engineered selective adaptation

After source training, detached module representations from the five encoders and both fusion blocks were scored using source-validation and target-adaptation data. Marginal domain evidence was biased multi-kernel MMD² after pooled label-blind RMS scaling, capped at 128 observations per domain, with 199 deterministic count-preserving label permutations. Predictive evidence was a signed source-relative class-centroid retention statistic, with bootstrap uncertainty. In the frozen `calibrated_logical` rule, a module adapts when its target retention upper percentile is below 0.75, **or** when permutation domain p ≤ 0.05, signed retention < 0.8 and its upper percentile < 1. Invalid or insufficient class geometry produces an explicit **ABSTAIN → SHARE**. The seven candidate modules decide independently, while the head is always updated. These p values are internal selector calibration statistics, not clinical inference tests; no multiple-module clinical claim follows from them.

The legacy top-k rule instead uses independently min-max-normalized MMD and target discriminability, `0.65 × MMD + 0.35 × (1 − discriminability)`, then adapts four of seven ranked modules. Calibrated threshold is the frozen related rule with its own thresholds. Fixed-mask tuning updates both fusion blocks and the head. Parameter-matched random uses the frozen closest-budget random-mask policy without forcing a nonempty mask. Its 0.5%-of-total-parameters tolerance did not ensure diverse masks in this small network. The closed internal method specification and 6B registration retain exact equations, versions and seeds; this release provides a [method and reproducibility summary](PHASE1_METHOD_AND_REPRODUCIBILITY.md), not those private/internal records.

## 6. Fixed experimental protocol and comparators

The target split was locked once with seed `20261025`; no subsequent partition was sought. Ten primary draws of **25 positives + 75 negatives** and ten nested low-label draws of **10 + 30** were stored in the private lock. Every target-adapted comparator used the corresponding identical draw. Source-only methods used no target adaptation labels. Target validation labels were additional to the adaptation budget and were used for permitted early stopping and development reporting, not adaptation fitting or selector scoring. Its 13 events make checkpoint choice and development rankings uncertain; the same validation people served early stopping and reported validation performance. Neural methods used the predeclared AdamW settings (learning rate 0.001, weight decay 0.0001, batch size 64, up to 30 source or 20 target epochs, patience 5). No post-test training, threshold tuning, recalibration or model selection was permitted.

The registered inventory contains source logistic regression, XGBoost, flat MLP and source five-view network; target logistic regression and target-only five-view network; head-only and full fine-tuning; fixed fusion mask; matched random mask; legacy top-k; calibrated threshold; and calibrated logical. The four source-only models used source-training labels. Target-only models used the corresponding adaptation-draw labels and no source-training labels. Transfer models used the frozen source initialization and exactly the same corresponding target draw. The last method is the **prespecified primary DMV-MAT-style method**, with full fine-tuning its primary comparator, regardless of development ranking. In total, **184 frozen checkpoint bindings** were evaluated: four source-only, 80 baseline/adaptation, and 100 selector fits. No comparison here relies on a favorable repetition chosen after validation or test inspection.

## 7. Outcomes, estimands and uncertainty

Primary performance is scikit-learn average precision, computed separately for each of ten paired primary-budget model draws on the same 283 locked MHAS people. The primary estimand is the arithmetic mean of ten within-draw AP differences: calibrated logical minus full fine-tuning. Secondary contrasts are logical minus head-only, matched random and source-only five-view. AUROC and Brier score are secondary. The low-label budget is secondary, and other methods are descriptive. No test threshold metric or fitted calibration model was used.

The frozen uncertainty procedure resampled **test participants jointly across every method and draw** 2,000 times, seed `20261026`, then computed per-draw metrics and their mean. Descriptive 2.5th/97.5th percentile intervals retain pairing and shared test membership. The ten overlapping adaptation draws are *not* ten independent test cohorts, and these intervals do not quantify uncertainty over other sites, cohorts, outcome definitions or training protocols. Zero registered replicates were invalid for the reported AP, AUROC or Brier analyses. No formal superiority or equivalence test was registered. Intervals do not repair label misclassification, outcome-observation selection or cross-country transportability.

## 8. Development validation and held-out results

The 6B development stage gave calibrated logical mean AP **0.1840**, full **0.1878**, head-only **0.1859**, and matched random **0.1847** at 25+75 labels, on 171 validation people with 13 positive reports. At 10+30, these were **0.1769**, **0.2044**, **0.1788**, and **0.1769**. Thus the primary selector had **no demonstrated development AP advantage**. [Figure 3](../figures/03_validation_ap.svg) shows these development values; they were not used to replace the preregistered primary selector.

On the held-out test, the prespecified 25+75 logical-minus-full AP difference was **+0.001819**, descriptive 95% interval **−0.024809 to +0.032340**. Mean AP was 0.142664 for logical and 0.140845 for full; mean AUROC was 0.702854 and 0.685714, and Brier was 0.067654 and 0.067472, respectively (lower Brier is better). The logical-minus-head AP difference was +0.000288 (−0.000707, +0.001350); logical-minus-random was +0.000841 (−0.000120, +0.002669). These intervals include zero. Source-only five-view AP was 0.135493; logical-minus-source AP was +0.007172 (−0.003773, +0.035074). The observed test event fraction was 21/283 (7.42%); AP must be interpreted in that context. **Table 2** gives the method summaries. [Figure 4](../figures/04_final_test_ap.svg) shows separate descriptive method intervals, **not** the paired primary-difference interval, which is the appropriate registered comparison. The complete 13-method AP/AUROC/Brier tables, metric intervals and per-draw aggregate variation are in [Final results](SUPPLEMENTARY_AGGREGATE_RESULTS.md).

## 9. Selector diagnostics and failure mechanisms

In nine of ten draws per budget, calibrated logical selected **no module beyond the head**. It selected haemodynamic once at 25+75 and fusion1 once at 10+30. Four modules—background, metabolic, behaviour and comorbidity/medication—had `uncertain_source_geometry` in all ten draws at each budget and therefore abstained under the frozen rule. The small source-validation positive count (21) is one plausible contributor to imprecise source-relative geometry; the recorded diagnostic alone does not identify a unique cause or demonstrate that those views lack clinical predictive value. The logical selector's mean pairwise mask Jaccard was 0.800 at both budgets, largely reflecting nine identical all-SHARE masks; stability did not improve from 10 to 25 positive adaptation labels in this real-data study. Domain MMD evidence often indicated marginal differences, but the frozen logical rule did not force adaptation from MMD alone.

At the primary budget, logical trained a mean **41/8,033 parameters (0.51%)**, compared with 33 (0.41%) for head-only and all 8,033 for full fine-tuning. Its sparse fraction thus mostly reflects behaving like head-only, not identified beneficial module adaptation. At 10+30, one fusion1 selection raised mean logical trainable fraction to **7.02%**, despite nine head-only-like masks. Fixed and legacy methods adapted approximately 93–96% of parameters at the primary budget because fusion blocks dominate. The matched-random mask equalled logical in **9/10 primary and 10/10 low-label draws**; the parameter-budget search space often admitted only one feasible mask. In the sole differing primary draw, the closest feasible random budget differed by 32 trainable parameters. This degeneracy limits the control's ability to isolate the value of *which* module was chosen. [Figure 5](../figures/05_parameters_vs_test_ap.svg) and [Figure 6](../figures/06_module_selection_frequency.svg) display the parameter/selection pattern; figure 5 compares *trainable parameter count*, not end-to-end runtime or energy. Selector scoring and calibration also consume resources.

Across primary draws, the logical test AP ranged 0.13687–0.14548 (SD 0.00271), while full fine-tuning ranged 0.12521–0.16822 (SD 0.01282). These are correlated adaptation-draw outcomes on a fixed test, not cross-sample uncertainty estimates. Four of ten full primary draws had AP below the source-only five-view AP, versus none of the logical draws, but this descriptive count cannot be generalized as a population negative-transfer rate or turned into an unregistered primary claim. The small positive point contrast cannot support superiority, equivalence, or proof of no possible benefit.

## 10. Runtime incident and transparent recovery

The first separately authorized final-test attempt **accessed MHAS test rows** and then stalled during a PyTorch/OpenMP LayerNorm inference path. It produced **no predictions or performance metrics**. Its private failure evidence and consumed authorization remain unchanged [11]. On invented tensors and all frozen checkpoint families, a later investigation reproduced a two-thread stall, found multiple OpenMP runtime copies, and validated separate, single-thread tabular and Torch worker processes [12]. These tests support the operational remedy but do not isolate a unique internal cause of the first native stall; LayerNorm was the observed stack location, not a proved defective mathematical operation.

A separately versioned recovery executor retained the same frozen registration, models, masks, preprocessing, participants, metrics and bootstrap. It was tested on synthetic data and then received a **new explicit user authorization** for exactly one recovery access to the same locked test. The recovery completed 184 aligned inference outputs and the registered statistics in a distinct private directory [13,14]. No model was retrained or selected from test performance. The episode is reported as a *failed first attempt followed by one authorized recovery*, not as an untouched first execution. [Figure 7](../figures/07_audit_timeline.svg) shows the audit sequence. The recovery process changed execution isolation and private routing, not the scientific estimand.

## 11. Discussion

The primary test point estimate slightly favored calibrated logical, while its registered interval was compatible with differences in either direction. The development stage favored full fine-tuning by a small mean AP margin at 25+75 and a larger margin at 10+30; the test did not reproduce that ranking. The correct scientific reading is **inconclusive evidence about predictive benefit**, combined with a clear observation that the implemented selector mostly abstained and that the matched-random control lacked independent mask diversity. The results neither demonstrate superiority nor establish equivalence of selective and full adaptation. Because the logical method frequently behaved like head-only tuning, the present test weakly identifies any benefit from *selective module choice* itself.

The synthetic Milestone-3 validation established that variable-size masks are technically coherent under controlled shifts, including zero-adaptation cases. The real public-cohort result tests a different question: whether those decisions help this HAALSI→MHAS endpoint with sparse target labels. Repeated all-SHARE decisions and uncertain source geometry are observations; scarcity of class examples as the reason for them is a plausible but untested explanation. Determining a causal mechanism would require a separately designed study. No selector equation is revised retrospectively. The current contribution is therefore an auditable transfer-learning case study with a negative/inconclusive primary finding, not an established algorithmic advance over DMV-MAT, domain adaptation or selective/parameter-efficient fine-tuning [1,15–18].

## 12. Limitations

First, the outcome is a **wave-observed, doctor-reported heart attack or stroke**. Neither disease component was adjudicated from records in these files, exact first-onset dates are generally unavailable, and an event between MHAS interview and examination could be misclassified relative to the landmark. MHAS positive carry-forward reflects harmonization rather than independent confirmation; HAALSI retraction handling reduces some contradiction but does not adjudicate disease. Self-report validity varies across external studies and may differ across South Africa and Mexico [9,10]. The sample is conditional on observed follow-up; deaths and noninterviews without an earlier positive were not coded as negatives, so survival and response selection may distort apparent performance.

Second, predictor comparability is imperfect: diabetes questions/derivations differ, assay platforms and lipids differ, and a baseline 0–30-day window does not eliminate timing ambiguity. The nine-feature diabetes-omitted, 0–14-day window and outcome-definition sensitivities were planned but **not trained or evaluated under this final registration**; they cannot be presented as completed supporting evidence. The 0–14-day inherited intersection had only eight validation and eleven test positive reports, below prespecified main floors.

Third, the source model fitted on 65 source-training positive reports, source-validation geometry used 21, target validation had 13, and the test had 21. AP and calibration estimates are imprecise, as the broad intervals show. Validation served both early stopping and development reporting, so the displayed validation metrics are not independent estimates of the selected checkpoints' generalization. Ten target adaptation draws overlap and share validation/test people. Both adaptation budgets were intentionally enriched to **25% positive**, unlike the locked test's **7.42% observed prevalence**; uncorrected probabilities may be miscalibrated. Brier scores are reported but do not establish calibration, and the sparse data do not justify a strong calibration curve, slope or clinical-risk claim. The study did not establish subgroup fairness or transportability to other settings. TRIPOD+AI and PROBAST+AI make these reporting and applicability issues salient [2,3]; we have not completed a formal checklist or risk-of-bias assessment.

Fourth, the selector's class-geometry abstentions and the degenerate random-mask matching narrow what can be inferred about module importance. High mask agreement mainly reflects all-SHARE repetition. A low trainable-parameter fraction does not measure total compute or prove a beneficial selective mechanism. The study has one source–target pairing, one frozen test population, and no completed nine-feature or 0–14-day test sensitivity; no claim about robustness to those design choices is possible. Finally, the first test attempt's access and technical failure preclude describing the recovery as a pristine first run, even though no result was available to inform model choice.

## 13. Conclusion

For the frozen HAALSI→MHAS public-study configuration, the re-engineered DMV-MAT-style calibrated-logical selector trained far fewer parameters than full fine-tuning, mostly by abstaining from module adaptation. Its registered held-out AP difference versus full fine-tuning was small and imprecise. The experiment **does not establish predictive superiority, equivalence, clinical incidence prediction or individual-risk validity**. Its contribution is a transparent, reproducible methodological comparison—including an unfavorable development finding, limited control interpretability, and disclosed runtime recovery—on a clearly defined survey-reported endpoint.
