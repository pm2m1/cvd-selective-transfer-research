# Scientific limitations and unperformed work

[Phase 1 index](overview.md) · [Original manuscript discussion](../FINAL_SUBMISSION_MANUSCRIPT.md)

The primary paired interval spans effects in both directions. Neither superiority nor equivalence follows. An AUROC near 0.70 does not establish clinical usefulness, good fixed-threshold sensitivity or calibrated individual risk. AP is neither accuracy nor precision at one threshold.

The outcome is a wave-observed survey report conditional on follow-up, subject to diagnosis access, recall, contradictory reports and attrition selection. Biomarker completeness and baseline restrictions also limit generalizability. Cross-country feature harmonization does not make measurements or ascertainment identical. No adjudicated onset dates, clinical decision utility or deployment validation are established.

The target validation set has 13 positive reports and the test has 21. Development stopping and reported validation performance share labels. Ten overlapping adaptation draws share one test population. Joint bootstrap intervals preserve pairing but cannot remove sampling, transportability or outcome-definition limitations. At fixed scores, a cutoff only changes decisions, not AP, AUROC or Brier.

Nine of ten primary logical masks were head-only apart from the always-updated head. Several clinical views lacked stable source geometry; abstention is not evidence that these views are medically irrelevant. Parameter-matched random masks often coincided with logical masks, so that control provided limited independent evidence. Small trainable fractions measure computational updating, not predictive superiority.

The enriched 25% adaptation prevalence differs from the observed test fraction. No probability recalibrator was fitted. A high all-negative accuracy would not demonstrate event detection. A retrospective test threshold sweep cannot establish an optimal clinical cutoff. Original authenticated validation probabilities have not been established as available; they are not reconstructed here.

The 0–14-day examination sensitivity was deferred for insufficient inherited event support. The nine-feature diabetes-omitted and alternative-endpoint configurations had no frozen final-test models. Their test results do not exist. Real-data cohort reconstruction, final inference and later exploratory clinical experiments are outside the public synthetic reproduction. The subsequent HRS → ELSA effort remains scientifically blocked despite working synthetic infrastructure.

A credible next study needs prespecified label accounting, separate training-side stopping/calibration, genuinely independent evaluation and adequate endpoint ascertainment. No further reuse of the already examined MHAS test for optimization is proposed.
