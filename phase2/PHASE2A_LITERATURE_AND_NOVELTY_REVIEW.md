# Phase 2A literature and novelty review

**Search boundary (26 October 2026).** This is a focused review of original papers, official proceedings and official study documentation, not a systematic review. Prior art must be checked again before any novelty claim. The finished HAALSI→MHAS result is diagnostic background only; its observed test performance cannot select a Phase-2 algorithm.

| Area | Established evidence | Consequence for Phase 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Regularized logistic models and clinical model updating | Elastic net combines coefficient shrinkage and selection [Zou & Hastie, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2005.00503.x). [Clinical prediction model updating guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9742671/) describes recalibration and coefficient updating as established approaches. | A target-only elastic-net model, source-only model, intercept-only recalibration and simple coefficient updating are strong mandatory controls. Source-centred regularization is not itself novel. |
| Gradient-boosted trees | [XGBoost](https://doi.org/10.1145/2939672.2939785) is an established tabular baseline. Tree boosting may capture nonlinear effects without multiview neural layers. | Predeclare modest, equally tuned boosting baselines. A new neural method must compete with them on identical rows/features/label budgets. |
| Classical domain adaptation | [CORAL](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10306) aligns source/target second-order feature statistics without target labels. Such alignment does not guarantee preservation of the outcome mechanism and may fail under concept or label shift. | Treat CORAL as a baseline when unlabeled **development** target covariates are legitimately available; never fit it on a held-out test. A covariance-matching step is not novel. |
| Fine-tuning and soft parameter constraints | Head-only/full fine-tuning are basic controls. [L2-SP](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/li18a.html) penalizes deviation from source pretrained weights. [Surgical fine-tuning](https://openreview.net/forum?id=APuPRxjHvZ) studies selective layer updating under shift. [Regularized multi-task learning](https://aima.cs.berkeley.edu/~russell/classes/cs294/f05/papers/evgeniou%2Bpontil-2004.pdf) already couples tasks through parameter regularization. | Softly adapting parameters or selecting layers/views has substantial prior art. A view-specific shrinkage method needs a precise new statistical question and convincing ablations; simply combining these techniques is not a new algorithm. |
| Parameter-efficient and multiview transfer | The original [DMV-MAT article](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNNLS.2024.3350085) addresses EEG multiview module adaptation. [Adapter modules](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/houlsby19a.html) and [SpotTune](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Guo_SpotTune_Transfer_Learning_Through_Adaptive_Fine-Tuning_CVPR_2019_paper.html) show existing parameter-efficient and adaptive tuning. | The five-view representation and a SHARE/ADAPT decision are not, by themselves, novelty. The original project's selector was a **re-engineered** rule, not a reproduction of the paper's exact formula. |
| Low-label uncertainty | With few positive labels, estimated class geometry, calibration and model selection are noisy. Source-relative geometry in the original study was invalid/uncertain for four modules in every draw. This is direct project evidence, not a general theorem. | An uncertainty-aware method should expose an abstention or continuous-shrinkage rule, report its calibration and label cost, and benchmark against fixed shrinkage. Do not create complex pseudo-label machinery before confirming data sufficiency. |
| External validation and reporting | [Vergouwe et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15845334/) proposed at least 100 events and 100 nonevents as an older starting point, while [Riley et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.9025) derive context-specific minimum sample sizes from desired precision of calibration, discrimination and overall fit. [BMJ external-validation guidance](https://www.bmj.com/content/384/bmj-2023-074821) emphasizes precision rather than a universal count. | Use 100 events/100 nonevents only as a **screen**, not an adequacy certificate. A target evaluation with 21 events, as in the original study, is too imprecise to establish small AP differences. Plan the actual sample size from anticipated prevalence, score distributions, AP/AUROC/calibration precision and the paired contrast before locking a new test. |

## Where a contribution could still be defensible

The strongest research question is **how much source information to retain by medically defined feature group when target labels are scarce and outcome/measurement shift is plausible**. A transparent, uncertainty-aware *continuous* shrinkage rule could address the original all-SHARE abstention without making an unreliable binary mask decision. This is a **candidate methodological contribution**, not a demonstrated novelty claim. It must be compared with global source-centred shrinkage, model updating, head-only/full fine-tuning, CORAL and strong tabular models; any added benefit must survive a separate, adequately sized external test. The method should be judged on AP and calibration/negative transfer as well as parameter count, not on selection frequency alone.

An equally plausible outcome is that regularized logistic regression or boosting remains better. In that case the honest contribution is a rigorous cross-population benchmark and failure analysis, not a new transfer algorithm. The original neural result provides no basis to assume a neural method will win.

## Bibliographic status

Links above lead to publisher, proceedings, paper or official author records. The clinical-update guidance is a methodological review; it establishes that updating is prior art, not that a particular update will help this study. No claim of exhaustive novelty clearance is made. Before submission, search domain-specific coefficient transport, Bayesian hierarchical shrinkage, group-wise transfer and survey-based CVD prediction systematically, then record inclusion criteria and exact bibliographic metadata.
